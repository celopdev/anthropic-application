# Why Anthropic — Celestin Prud'homme

I joined Skello at the very beginning because I believed in the product. I had managed restaurants and lived the pain of missing scheduling tools firsthand. When I heard what they were building, I didn't need convincing.

I have the same feeling about Anthropic.

The difference is scale. Anthropic isn't building a scheduling tool for one industry — it's building infrastructure for how humans and AI work together. That matters at a level I haven't felt since joining Skello.

The CI/CD role specifically: fast, reliable CI is the nervous system of a high-performing engineering org. At a company shipping frontier AI models with real safety implications, that nervous system matters more than anywhere else. I want to be the person who makes it work.

I'm also an autodidact. I believe in learning by doing, by caring, by throwing yourself into things that matter. That's how I grew at Skello. That's how I want to grow at Anthropic.

---

## Why the CI/CD role specifically

CI/CD for most companies is a convenience. At Anthropic, it's something different.

When you're shipping models that have real-world consequences, the systems that let you do that safely and quickly aren't support infrastructure — they're safety infrastructure. A flaky test that gets silenced, a pipeline that skips a check, a deployment that bypasses review — these things have different stakes at a frontier AI company than they do anywhere else. The CI team at Anthropic isn't keeping the lights on. It's part of how the work gets done responsibly.

That's the version of developer productivity I want to build. Not faster builds for a scheduling app (though I love that work). Faster, safer, more auditable builds for engineers doing something that genuinely matters for how AI develops.

---

## Values alignment

Anthropic talks about safety, responsibility, long-term thinking. These aren't just words to me.

I spent 8 years at one company. I think in decades, not quarters. I care about the downstream effects of technical decisions — not just "does this work now" but "what does this decision create later." I care about building things that are reliable and honest — in the code and in the culture.

I'm also someone who can hold both sides of a difficult thing. I'm genuinely excited about AI. I'm also clear-eyed about why the safety work matters. That combination — enthusiasm without naivety — is how I've tried to approach big technical decisions at Skello too. Migrate the CI, but don't break what's already working. Move fast, but know exactly what you're trading off.

I'm an autodidact. I learned to code in 3 months and built a career from it because I was willing to learn relentlessly and take problems seriously. That's still how I operate.

---

## What I'd bring

I've spent years making other engineers' lives better. CI pipelines, tooling, documentation, pairing, architecture guidance. It's not a side effect of my role — it's the role.

I know how to:
- Migrate complex CI infrastructure without losing your team
- Build test parallelization systems that actually balance load
- Make developers owners of the systems they ship
- Build internal AI tooling that engineers actually use

Now I want to do that for an engineering org whose work I believe in.
